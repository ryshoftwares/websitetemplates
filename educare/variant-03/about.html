Our history

Founded as a teachers' college. Grown into a full institute.

Oakridge Institute opened its doors in 1901 as a small teachers' college in rural New Hampshire. Over the next twelve decades it grew into a full institute of arts, sciences and business — without ever abandoning its founding commitment to small classes.

Our mission

Serious scholarship, taught at human scale.

Oakridge Institute exists to provide a rigorous liberal arts and professional education inside classes small enough for real discussion, real mentorship, and real accountability — the conditions we believe produce serious thinkers and capable professionals alike.

A timeline

125 years, six defining moments.

1901

Founded as Oakridge Normal School

Opens with 40 students training as teachers, in a single academic hall that still stands on campus today.

1938

Becomes a four-year liberal arts institute

Expands its charter to grant Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Science degrees across history, literature and the sciences.

1965

First graduate programs

Introduces its first graduate degree, the Master of Education, to serve working teachers across the region.

1998

Business program established

Launches the Bachelor of Business Administration and Bachelor of Arts in Economics as full four-year majors.

2010

The Ferris Science Building opens

A new science building gives the Computer Science and Environmental Studies programs dedicated labs and research space.

2021

Master of Public Policy launched

Adds a two-year graduate policy program, taught by faculty with direct government and nonprofit experience.

What guides us

Four commitments that haven't changed since 1901.

Small by design

Class sizes are capped deliberately, not as a marketing point but as an academic strategy.

Faculty who teach

Every professor teaches undergraduates directly — there are no teaching assistants standing in.

Rigor over trend

We add programs slowly and only when we can staff them properly, not to chase enrollment.

Access matters

Need-based financial aid covers a meaningful share of tuition for most admitted students.

Come walk the same halls our founders built.

Campus tours run weekday mornings, with time built in to sit in on an active class.
